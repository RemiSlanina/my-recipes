# The Bread Hunt

## What I Was Looking For

For many years I tried to recreate the bread I remembered from Croatia and Italy.

The target was a light white loaf with a soft crumb, large irregular holes, and a texture that felt different from most breads available to me locally.

I later learned that this style of bread is often called "filone" in Italy.

## Early Assumptions

I initially assumed the difference came from:

- sourdough
- yeast type
- kneading technique
- fermentation time

Over time I became increasingly suspicious that flour might be the missing variable.

My mother repeatedly claimed that Croatian and Italian bread used a finer flour.

## Experiments

- Whole wheat bread
- White wheat bread
- Sourdough
- Commercial yeast
- Bread machine baking
- Oven baking
- Reduced salt versions
- Seed breads

Results varied, but none reproduced the texture I remembered.

## Later Discovery

I eventually found references linking Tipo 00 flour with certain Italian breads, including some filoni, rosette, and pizza doughs.

This was notable because Tipo 00 flour had been difficult to obtain locally for many years. 

In Croatia, Tipo 00 is widely used from what I learned, though it has no specific name (I got told it's just the general flour). 

The discovery appeared to support the hypothesis that flour choice was playing a larger role than I had previously assumed. 

Since I cannot confirm local flour status, I have to wait until I have an oven and try to achieve a satisfying crumb.
